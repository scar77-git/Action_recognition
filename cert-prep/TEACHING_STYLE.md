# How lessons are taught (learner's agreed preferences)

1. **No unexplained abbreviations.** Spell out every term the first time it appears in a lesson,
   with a one-line plain-English meaning — e.g. "SCP (Service Control Policy): an organisation-wide
   rule that caps what any account can do". Each day's PDF ends with a glossary.
2. **Explain before tabulating.** Each concept gets a proper explanation in prose first: what it is,
   why it exists, how it works. Tables only summarise what has already been explained.
3. **Practical examples** for every key concept — a concrete, everyday scenario showing it in use.
4. **Diagrams** (ASCII) wherever a flow, architecture or relationship can be drawn.
5. **Paced in parts.** A day is split into 2–3 parts. After each part, stop and ask whether anything
   is unclear; continue only when the learner says so.
6. **Warm-up / quiz questions come only after** the whole day's topic and the lab are finished.
7. **Understanding over speed.** Prefer depth on key concepts over covering more ground quickly.
